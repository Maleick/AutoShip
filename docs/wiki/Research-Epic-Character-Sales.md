# Research: Epic Character Sales Infrastructure

*Research date: 2026-04-15*

This note answers `TextQuest#1589` using public Teek market data from 2024-2026 as the closest public analog for a free-trade, box-friendly TLP. The core conclusion matches Jeff's 2026-04-14 session note: on box-friendly servers, finished epic characters decay in value faster than epic MQ inventory, so the repeatable business is usually "pre-staged shell + bottleneck piece + same-day MQ sale", not "warehouse finished characters and hope the right buyer appears."

---

## Executive Summary

- **MQ beats finished-character resale for repeatable cash flow.** Teek public listings show monk, mage, and bard MQ packages repeatedly listing between `8-20 KR`, while finished epic characters for those same classes later compress toward `4-8 KR` equivalent.
- **Cleric is the only class that still supports occasional finished-character inventory.** Public Teek sales show epic clerics still clearing around `10-18 KR` equivalent, with higher upside only when the account is clearly raid-ready / flagged, while open cleric MQ asks have already compressed into the low-single-digit KR band.
- **Do not hold a deep bench of completed epic characters.** Hold at most `1-2` finished sale-ready toons total, and only during the launch / early-expansion demand spike. Everything else should stay as leveled shells, keyed helpers, or bottleneck MQ kits.
- **Best inventory shape:** `0-1` finished cleric, `0` finished monks, `0` finished mages, `0` finished bards, plus `3-5` pre-staged MQ pipelines spread across monk / mage / bard / cleric.

---

## Working Assumptions

### Krono conversion baseline

Public Krono sale threads on ECTunnel in late 2024 and early 2025 clustered around roughly `$9-10.25` per Krono, so all cash-denominated account prices below are normalized with a working conversion of `1 KR ~= $10`.[1][2]

### Why Teek is the primary pricing proxy

Teek exposes the closest public market shape to the target business model:

- free-trade item movement
- strong boxing / alt demand
- visible ECTunnel MQ and account-sale activity
- enough public sale titles to infer price compression over time

Fresh public 2026 pricing is strongest for **cleric MQ** and the generic Krono cash rate. Bard, mage, and monk rely more heavily on the 2024-2025 Teek decay curve because public 2026 titles are much thinner. Where the sample is thin, the recommendations below intentionally prefer conservative execution bands over optimistic ask prices.

### Practical interpretation

- **Best MQ classes:** monk, mage, bard
- **Best finished-toon class:** cleric
- **Worst finished-toon hold:** monk
- **Best "sell now" rule:** if a monk, mage, or bard epic bottleneck drops while demand is hot, sell the MQ immediately instead of finishing and parking the character

## Pipeline Design: Level -> Epic -> Sell

This is the recommended production flow for a 36-box operator who wants repeatable sales instead of trophy characters.

### Phase 1: Build reusable shells

1. Power-level target classes to the minimum practical epic-ready floor.
2. Stop treating the shell as the inventory item; the shell is only a reusable chassis.
3. Keep the shell parked at the last non-binding step until a buyer or bottleneck opportunity appears.

### Phase 2: Pre-stage access and helpers

1. Solve any zone access, Chardok royal access, Plane of Sky access, or Kedge handling before a sale window opens.
2. Keep at least one helper roster that can finish the "awkward" step on demand.
3. Favor reusable logistics over finished characters. Access and control are the real moat.

### Phase 3: Convert bottlenecks into same-day sales

1. When the bottleneck piece drops, decide immediately whether the market is hot enough for MQ.
2. If the class is monk, mage, or bard, default to **same-day MQ** unless a pre-arranged buyer is paying a clean-character premium.
3. If the class is cleric, compare the MQ bid to the value of a clean finished toon. Cleric is the one class where finishing the character can still be rational.

### Phase 4: Recycle the shell

1. Complete the trade.
2. Rebuild the bottleneck kit, not the entire character inventory.
3. Restock whichever of the four epic classes is currently shortest in public listings.

### Estimated wall-clock throughput

These are operator estimates, assuming an established boxing stack and no catastrophic camp competition:

| Class | Leveling shell | Epic prep after shell exists | Sale motion | Total cycle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cleric | `1-2 days` | `1-4 days` depending on Chardok / Gimblox luck | Same-day MQ or `1-7 days` for premium character sale | `2-8 days` |
| Bard | `1-2 days` | `1-4 days` depending on Kedge / Sky logistics | Same-day MQ | `2-6 days` |
| Mage | `1-2 days` | `1-3 days` once elemental lines are controlled | Same-day MQ | `2-5 days` |
| Monk | `1-2 days` | `1-4 days` depending on robe / Raster / Xenevorash | Same-day MQ | `2-6 days` |

The key pattern is that **character-sale latency is usually worse than epic-completion latency**. MQ converts immediately; account sale requires buyer discovery, trust, and account handoff.

## Account Sale vs Character Sale Economics

In the public market, "character sale" is usually still an **account credential sale with one hero toon highlighted in the title**. That matters because the account shell starts carrying most of the risk and some of the value.

| Sale format | Revenue ceiling | Time to cash | Operational risk | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MQ / epic service | Medium to high (`2-20 KR` depending on class and server age) | Fastest | Low to medium | Repeatable weekly cash flow |
| Single epic toon on its own account | Medium (`4-30 KR`, but highly class- and timing-dependent) | Medium | Medium | Opportunistic flips, mostly cleric |
| Whole account bundle with multiple raid-ready toons / personas | Highest absolute ceiling | Slowest | Highest | End-of-cycle liquidation, not recurring farm income |

### What the public data says

- **Monk and mage**: the MQ package can equal or exceed the entire toon's resale value.[9][11][12][13]
- **Bard**: a launch-rush buyer may pay a premium for the finished toon, but that premium erodes hard once leveling catches up.[3][7][8]
- **Cleric**: the finished toon can still outperform MQ, but only when the account is easy to transfer and the buyer is clearly shopping for a raid-ready priest, not just the epic.[4][5][6]

### Strategic conclusion

Use **MQ for recurring income** and **account sales only for liquidation or premium cleric buyers**. Finished-toon inventory is a spot trade, not the base business.

---

## Inventory Recommendation

### Optimal finished-character inventory

Hold **`1-2` finished epic characters max**.

- **Slot 1:** one premium cleric if the account is clean and the market still pays a raid-ready premium
- **Slot 2:** one optional launch-window bard or mage only if buyer traffic is already present

Do **not** stock finished monks as inventory. Public pricing says the market treats them like disposable throughput, not premium assets.

### Optimal MQ pipeline inventory

Hold **`3-5` MQ pipelines** instead of finished toons:

- `2x` monk-ready pipelines
- `1x` mage-ready pipeline
- `1x` bard-ready pipeline
- `0-1x` cleric-ready pipeline depending on current guild / raid demand

That mix matches both public market pricing and the real friction points:

- monk moves fast when the robe / Xenevorash problem is already solved
- mage sells when all four elemental headaches are already compressed into one appointment
- bard sells because buyers hate the Kedge / Sky logistics
- cleric is best kept flexible because it can become either an MQ or a premium finished-toon sale

### Reorder rule

If more than one finished toon sits unsold for a week, stop finishing characters and revert to shell + MQ inventory only.

---

## Final Recommendation

For a recurring-income operation, the durable infrastructure is:

1. leveled shells for cleric / bard / mage / monk
2. solved access, faction, and helper logistics
3. a standing bottleneck-piece pipeline
4. the discipline to sell MQ quickly instead of over-finishing characters

If the goal is reliable Krono velocity rather than one-off liquidation, **the business should be organized around epic MQ throughput, with cleric as the only recurring exception where a finished-character sale can still beat the MQ.**
